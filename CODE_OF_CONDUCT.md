# Code of conduct

This is a small project with one maintainer. The rules here are the ones that keep it
workable, not a policy written to be quoted back at people.

## Where it applies

Issues, pull requests, commit messages, discussions, the repository browser's pages, and any
place someone is representing the project. The project's own game servers and website are not
policed by this file; their moderation is a feature (see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md)), not a
conduct matter.

## What we expect

- **Report the thing you actually saw.** The version, the executable, the command, the log.
  "It doesn't work" costs a round trip that a `startup_trace.txt` would have saved.
- **Assume the maintainer is reading in good faith**, and extend the same in review. A
  rejected change is usually about scope, not about you.
- **Keep other people's data out of the thread.** No account tokens, no player data files, no
  someone else's IP from a log. Redact before you paste.
- **Say when you are unsure.** "I could not test this on a GPU" is useful. Silence that looks
  like a tested claim is not.
- **Stay on the topic of the issue.** Unrelated asks belong in their own issue, where they can
  be considered properly.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, threats, or following someone between threads after they have asked you to stop.
- Slurs, or hostility aimed at people for who they are rather than what they wrote.
- Sexual content, or anything sexual involving minors. This project imitates a platform used
  by children, and that fact is not an invitation.
- Publishing someone's private information, including their real name, address, or account
  credentials.
- Using the project to attack people: credential stuffing against other installs, scraping
  players' data out of an install you do not own, or shipping a build with something hidden in
  it.
- Help with cheating on, or evading detection on, installs you do not run yourself. The
  anti-cheat exists because servers cannot trust clients; asking for a bypass for someone
  else's server is out of scope, and asking about your own local build is fine.
- Generated text dumped into issues or reviews at volume, or comments that restate the diff.

## Reporting a problem

Contact the maintainer directly, [@xyz-elyxion](https://github.com/xyz-elyxion), by opening an
issue only if you are comfortable doing so in public. If the report is about a person, or you
would rather not be named, use GitHub's **Report abuse** form on the offending content instead;
that reaches GitHub's own reviewers.

Include what happened, where, links or screenshots if they help, and what outcome you are
looking for. You do not have to justify being upset before the report is taken seriously.

Retaliation against someone who reports in good faith is itself a breach of this file.

## What happens

The maintainer reads the report, asks for anything missing, and decides. Depending on what
happened, the response is: a note explaining what was wrong, deleting or editing the comment,
locking the thread, blocking the account from the repository, or reporting it to GitHub. There
is no committee, no formal hearing and no appeal process beyond asking once more, with
information you think was missed.

Reports about security vulnerabilities are not conduct matters and have their own route in
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md). Reports about the project's licensing belong in an issue.
